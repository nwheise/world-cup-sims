# CLAUDE.md — World Cup 2026 Seattle Match Simulator

## What this project is

This project assumes tickets to two FIFA World Cup 2026 knockout matches at Lumen Field, Seattle:

- **Match 82** — Round of 32, **Wed July 1, 2026, 13:00 PT**: Group G winner vs 3rd place from Group A/E/H/I/J
- **Match 94** — Round of 16, **Mon July 6, 2026, 17:00 PT**: Winner of Match 81 vs Winner of Match 82
  - **Match 81** — Round of 32, July 1, Levi's Stadium (SF Bay Area): Group D winner vs 3rd place from Group B/E/F/I/J

Goal: Monte Carlo simulation to estimate which teams will appear in those two matches, so a
ticketholder knows which group-stage outcomes to root for to maximize the chance of seeing
marquee teams live.

Tournament runs June 11 – July 19, 2026. Group stage ends June 27. **As of June 11 (project
start), zero group matches had been played** — the sim simulates all 72 group games from ratings.

### Official ranking rules (FIFA 2026 regulations — these changed from prior World Cups)

- **Within-group tiebreakers, in order**: points; then **head-to-head first** among tied teams
  (H2H points, H2H GD, H2H goals — new for 2026, UEFA-style); then overall GD, overall goals;
  then team conduct score (cards: yellow −1, second-yellow red −3, direct red −4, yellow+direct
  red −5); then FIFA World Ranking.
- **Ranking of the 12 third-placed teams** (best 8 advance): points → GD → goals scored →
  team conduct score → FIFA World Ranking. **No drawing of lots in 2026** (removed from
  regulations; FIFA ranking is the final criterion).
- Third-place teams are assigned to bracket slots by the **fixed Annex C lookup table** —
  no second draw, no discretion. R32 never rematches group-stage opponents.

## Model details (`seattle_wc_sim.py`)

- **Ratings** (`RATINGS` dict): FIFA points from the **April 1, 2026** release for the
  published top ~20; everything else is an estimate (marked in comments). Some values have
  fractional offsets (e.g. 1673.07) purely to keep the FIFA-ranking tiebreaker deterministic.
  Ratings only enter via pairwise differences. **Improvement candidate**: replace with current
  Elo from eloratings.net, or the final pre-tournament FIFA ranking (released June 9-11, 2026 —
  Argentina reclaimed #1 just before kickoff; the April list had France #1, Spain #2,
  Argentina #3).
- **Match model**: Elo expectation `E = 1/(1+10^(-diff/600))` (FIFA's 600 scale); goals are
  independent Poisson with `λ_a = 2.7·E`, `λ_b = 2.7·(1−E)`. Knockout ties broken with
  probability `0.5 + 0.4·(E−0.5)` as an ET/penalties proxy.
- **Conduct score is NOT simulated** (we don't model cards). It's skipped in both tiebreaker
  chains; the next criterion (FIFA ranking via `RATINGS`) resolves ties deterministically.
  Only matters on exact pts/GD/GF ties — negligible distortion, but worth stating honestly.
- Group ranking implements the full H2H-first algorithm including re-ranking within tied
  clusters (`rank_group`).

  the other eligible groups: H 26%, J 15%, I 12%, **E only 2%**. So the Seattle R32 opponent
  is overwhelmingly a Group A story; "Germany finishes 3rd and comes to Seattle" is ~dead

  (Per-combination counts, not probability-weighted, but the bias dominates.)
- **Match 94 appearance**: Belgium ~42%, **USA ~31%** (path: win Group D → win M81 → Seattle),
  Iran ~17%, Australia ~12%, Türkiye ~10%. Most likely matchup: **Belgium vs USA ~13%**.
- **Cheering guide**: (1) USA must *win* Group D — runner-up routes to Dallas, only the winner's
  path reaches Seattle; (2) Belgium to win Group G; (3) root for **Mexico to finish 3rd in
  Group A** (best realistic "cool opponent" outcome — Mexico–Belgium at Lumen), with Uruguay
  3rd in H as the secondary angle. Bonus: Egypt vs Iran is at Lumen June 26 (Match 63).

## Personalized preference workflow (`rank_prefs.py` → `cheer_guide.py`)

Turns the generic findings above into advice tailored to *your* taste in two steps:
1. `python3 rank_prefs.py` — rank teams by head-to-head picks → `preferences.json` (per-team weights).
2. `python3 cheer_guide.py` — for each of the 72 group games, which result to root for to maximize the
   chance your favorites reach Seattle.

Objective is *maximize total Seattle-lineup enjoyment, weighted toward favorites*: per simulated
tournament, `utility = sum(emphasize(weight) for each distinct Seattle team)` (the teams you'd watch across
both tickets — M82's two plus M94's other). `emphasize` is an anchored exponential `f(w) = (e^(γw)−1)/(e^γ−1)`
(f(0)=0, f(1)=1, convex), with `γ = EMPHASIS = 2.0` (module constant — edit to taste; γ=0 is the plain
linear sum). Summing rewards lineups with **multiple** favorites (USA *and* Brazil > USA *and* a minnow),
unlike the original max-weight objective which ignored the second team once your top pick was in; the
convex `emphasize` then tilts that sum so a beloved team outweighs several lukewarm ones (at γ=2 a
mid-table team ≈ 0.39 of a favorite, a minnow ≈ 0.07 — a gentle tilt). The guide buckets one large MC by each game's outcome —
valid as a causal effect because group games are independent given ratings — so it surfaces counterintuitive
calls automatically (e.g. if you like a Group H team whose only Seattle path is finishing **3rd**, it may
tell you to root for them to *lose* a game so they drop out of the top two). A 3σ noise floor hides games
swamped by sampling error.

**Loyalty guard** (added because the raw objective will gleefully tell you to cheer against your own
favorite): a team outside the two feeder-winner groups (D, G) can only reach Seattle by finishing 3rd, so
the utility-max move is often to root for it to *lose*. Whether that's good advice depends entirely on the
Annex C routing — a Group A third reaches Seattle ~95% of the time, a Group E third ~2%. So the guide also
buckets, per game and outcome, each participant's `P(reach Seattle | outcome)`. When the recommended result
roots against a team you like (weight ≥ `LIKE_FLOOR`, default 0.5), it computes the **swing** in that team's
Seattle odds between the recommended (losing) result and the result where they win their own match. If the
swing clears `SWING_THRESHOLD` (default 0.03 = 3pp) the call stays, annotated with the honest payoff
(`9% → 25% (+16pp)`); if not, it's demoted to a "LOYALTY NOTES" footnote (`just root for Mexico`) rather than
headlining the guide. Swing-based, not absolute, so it measures the leverage of *that one game*. Both
constants are module-level — edit to taste. `assess_loyalty()` is a pure function (unit-tested in
`TestLoyaltyGuard`). (Possible future tweak: weight M94 above M82 since the R16 match is the marquee one.)

## The website (`site/` — GitHub Pages, zero backend)

Generalizes the Python tools to ANY attended matches (all 104, not just Seattle's two), as a
static site: everything computes client-side, user state lives in localStorage, and the only
moving part is a scheduled GitHub Action.

- **Architecture**: `tournament.json` (static facts incl. the full bracket: R32 73–88, R16
  89–96 — M94 = W81 vs W82 in Seattle — QF 97–100, SF 101/102, 3rd-place 103, final 104; slot
  codes are openfootball-style `1A`/`2B`/`3A/E/H/I/J`/`W81`/`L101`) + `results.json` (live
  scores). The worker runs the MC once (default 20k sims, seed 42), storing per sim: all 72
  group outcomes, group positions, and every knockout match's participants + winner (~216
  bytes/sim packed). Preference/attendance changes are then instant re-aggregations
  ("analyze"), no re-simulation.
- **Live results**: played group games are pinned to their real scores; known knockout winners
  are honored when they match the sim's bracket lineup (guard against partial/stale feeds).
  Cheer rows cover unplayed group games + unplayed knockout games whose REAL participants are
  known. The preference pool auto-narrows to teams that can still reach the user's attended
  matches (it reproduces the 36-team `FEEDER_GROUPS` set when you select M82+M94 — emergent
  from the sim, not hardcoded).
- **Data source**: openfootball/worldcup.json (public domain, raw.githubusercontent.com, no
  key). Score schema: `score.ft`/`score.et`/`score.p` — winner = p over et over ft. Team-name
  mapping lives in `scripts/update_results.py` (and `build_data.py`).
- **Four tabs**: Cheer guide (impact-ranked recommendations + loyalty notes + noise-floor
  collapse), My matches (checkbox schedule, venue filter), My teams (head-to-head duels +
  ranking), Probabilities (per-slot knockout odds, group standings + advancement, champion).
- The browser smoke test was driven via headless Chrome + CDP; rendered numbers matched the
  Python findings. After JS changes run `node --test "tests/**/*.test.mjs"`.
