🕰 Time machine

See the odds as they stood after an earlier match: every probability re-derives using only the results played up to and including the match you pick.

How this works

  • Model: goals are Poisson draws scaled by an Elo expectation on FIFA's 600-point scale (avg 2.7 goals/match); knockout ties break with a small skill edge as an extra-time/penalties proxy. Ratings are the official FIFA points from the June 11, 2026 release, the last ranking before the tournament. No host advantage, no injuries.
  • Rules: official 2026 regulations end to end — head-to-head-first group tiebreakers, ranking of third-placed teams, and FIFA's real Annex C table (all 495 combinations) routing the best 8 thirds into the bracket.
  • Live results: actual scores are merged in as the tournament is played — refreshed about every hour from ESPN's public scoreboard (with the public-domain openfootball dataset as a fallback). Played games are pinned as fact; the simulation only fills in what hasn't happened yet, so every probability sharpens as the tournament goes.
  • Your preferences: teams start ranked by strength (the default assumption: you want to see the best teams); head-to-head picks personalize from there, and ⭐ pinned teams are locked above everything else. "Lineup quality" is the favorite-weighted appeal of the distinct teams you'd watch across your matches.
  • Cheer guide: one big simulation records every game's outcome and who ends up in your attended matches; bucketing by outcome gives the causal effect of each result on your expected lineup quality. A loyalty guard stops it from telling you to root against a team you love unless the payoff is real (for that team or another favorite) — and it will never tell you to cheer against a team you've pinned.
  • Must-watch: your team ranking becomes a personalized TV schedule — every remaining match scored by the expected appeal of the teams on the pitch (knockout slots weighted by who's likely to fill them), with one-tap export to your calendar (.ics).
  • Accuracy: the model also publishes a win/draw/loss probability for every match (shown on each game in My matches). The Accuracy tab grades those calls against real results with a reliability diagram that checks whether, say, the games called ~60% home win really do end that way about 60% of the time, plus a one-line summary of how much better than a random guess the calls have been. Scored underneath with the multiclass Brier score (0–2, lower is better). It's one tournament with correlated outcomes, and football is high-variance, so read it as descriptive, not a verdict.
  • Team path: follow one team and the same simulation scores every remaining game by how much its result extends their expected run — group survival, but also which bracket slots and opponents each result steers them toward. It will tell you honestly when a loss would route them somewhere friendlier, but it never tells you to cheer against your own team.
  • Privacy: everything runs in your browser; your picks live in localStorage and never leave your device. The only measurement is an anonymous, cookieless page-view count (GoatCounter).
